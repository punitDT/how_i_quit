Why walking works

Thinking it away does not work. Move instead.

A craving lasts a few minutes. Walking spends that window on movement instead of on fighting the thought, and the restlessness ends up somewhere to go other than a cigarette.

1Stand up now, before you talk yourself out of it.

2Leave the room. Different air, different place, different thought.

3Walk at a pace that raises your breathing a little.

4Stay out until the timer ends, then breathe.

The urge fades on its own. Slow the breath and stay with it.

A craving lasts a few minutes. Breathing spends that window on the exhale instead of on the urge, and the restlessness settles while you stay still.

1Breathe in easily, without filling more than you need.

2Let the exhale run a little longer than the inhale.

3Keep your attention on the exhale, or on a spot on the floor if that is easier.

4Stay with it until the timer ends.

Alternating your nostrils can deepen the slowdown, and it can slow things down faster during a craving. Skip it if a nostril is blocked or closing one is uncomfortable. Breathe through both, slowly, with your attention on the exhale. That focus is what settles you, not the pattern.

The timer runs in the hours you pick. While it is running, you don't smoke.

That is the whole rule, and the plan is built to make it easier to keep. The timer holds a little longer every day, so the gap between one cigarette and the next keeps widening until a long gap is the normal one.

1Pick the hours you usually smoke. Those are the hours the timer holds for you.

2It starts short on purpose. A short hold you keep beats a long one you don't.

3When a craving lands mid-hold, you don't smoke. Open Craving Escape and walk it out, then come back to the clock.

4Each day adds a little more time. After Day 7 it keeps running, and the rule stays exactly the same.
